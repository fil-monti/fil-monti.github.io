<Slide id="nondiag-s-contribution" class="cgsi-nondiag-slide">

## Inference of the drift matrix <span class="cgsi-nondiag-title-symbol">S</span>

<div class="cgsi-nondiag-content">

<div class="cgsi-nondiag-columns">

<div class="cgsi-nondiag-column cgsi-nondiag-left-column">
  <section class="cgsi-nondiag-section">

### Challenges

    <div class="cgsi-nondiag-copy">

(1) <span class="cgsi-nondiag-title-symbol">S</span> must be (Hurwitz) stable: $(\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \le 0)$

(2) computational complexity of branch variances(lots of $e^{tS}$);

    </div>
        <div class="cgsi-nondiag-equation cgsi-nondiag-schur-equation">

$$
\int_{0}^{t} e^{(t-\tau) S} \Sigma  e^{(t-\tau) S^{T}}   d\tau
$$

    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="cgsi-nondiag-section cgsi-nondiag-but-section">

*Current scalable solutions:* diagonal or symmetric parametrizations

    <p> </p>

**But**: they can only describe "monotone" evolution

    <p class="cgsi-nondiag-schur-label">Using Schur blocks:</p>
    <div class="cgsi-nondiag-equation cgsi-nondiag-schur-equation">

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha & \beta \\
-\beta & \alpha
\end{pmatrix}
$$

    </div>

**But**: need to pre-specify the number of complex conjugate pairs

  </section>
</div>

<div class="cgsi-nondiag-column cgsi-nondiag-right-column">
  <section class="cgsi-nondiag-section">

### Proposed parametrization

A matrix $S \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ is represented under the ***Hurwitz-smooth spectral block parametrization*** (**H-SSBP**) if it can be written as

  </section>

  <div class="cgsi-nondiag-equation cgsi-nondiag-main-equation">

$$
S = RBR^{-1},
$$

  </div>

  <p class="cgsi-nondiag-note">where $B = \operatorname{bdiag}(B_1,\ldots,B_{n_B})$ is block diagonal, with</p>
  <div class="cgsi-nondiag-equation cgsi-nondiag-block-equation">
$$
B_b = B(\rho_b,\theta_b,\omega_b) :=
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho_b \cos \theta_b & \rho_b \sin \theta_b + \omega_b \\
\rho_b \sin \theta_b - \omega_b & \rho_b \cos \theta_b
\end{pmatrix}
$$
  </div>
  <p class="cgsi-nondiag-note">
  If $K$ is odd, one block is scalar.<br />
  <strong class="cgsi-nondiag-blue-label">Smooth transition between coupled complex eigenvalues and decoupled real eigenvalues</strong><br />
  <strong class="cgsi-nondiag-blue-label">Stability</strong> is guaranteed by $\rho_b \lt 0, \theta_b \in [-\frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{4}]$<br />
  <strong class="cgsi-nondiag-blue-label">Matrix support</strong>: all matrices diagonalizable over $\mathbb{C}$ and some defective cases $\implies$ full Lebesgue measure over the stable cone
  </p>

</div>

</div>

</div>

<p class="cgsi-slide-citation">Monti, F., Holbrook, A., Glatt-Holtz, N. E., and Suchard, M. A. (2026+). <em>Stable Matrix Parametrizations and Structured Adjoints for Ornstein--Uhlenbeck Processes</em>.</p>

</Slide>

<h2>Mathematical model</h2>

The central object is the continuous-time covariance balance

$$
A\Sigma + \Sigma A^\top + Q = 0.
$$

If the drift admits a stable eigensystem, then

$$
\exp(tA) = R \exp(tB) R^{-1},
$$

which turns repeated propagation into a structured computational problem.
